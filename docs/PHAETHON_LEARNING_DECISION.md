# Should Phaethon Learn From Its Own Trades? — Decision Memo
**Date:** 2026-07-05 · responds to operator pushback on the frozen-policy-per-window rule
**Prior position:** policy frozen per cohort window; revisions only at boundaries via ceremony.
**This memo's obligation:** steelman the learning case first, then reckon honestly, then a verdict with its own kill criterion. No hedging into "more research needed" without naming the research and the settling result.

---

## Verdict (stated first, argued below)

**A specific narrow middle ground — and it is a genuine, partial change from my prior recommendation.** The operator's argument contains one thing that is right and one thing that is wrong, and they separate cleanly. Right: the review cadence should be set by **information accrual, not the calendar** — my prior design implicitly tied boundary reviews to long fixed windows, which wastes signal when trades close faster and (worse) invites reviews when almost no signal exists. Wrong: that *continuous or frequent* outcome-adjustment would make Phaethon better — at Phaethon's actual trade frequency the arithmetic says there is nothing statistically real to learn from at high frequency, so a continuous learner would be fitting noise with a fluent narrator attached, which is this operator's own 80-iterations failure mode running on a cron. The recommendation: **keep NO_LIVE_PNL_LEARNING's bright line, replace calendar-based boundaries with n-triggered boundaries (eligible at ≥25 closed trades since last review AND ≥13 weeks elapsed), and systematize the review input as a statistical "lessons ledger" with a per-lesson evidence bar, a two-lesson change budget per revision, forward predictions attached to every adopted lesson, and a pre-registered kill criterion for the learning mechanism itself.** This captures everything real in "the agent should learn" — calibration correction and thesis-category pruning at meta-level, which are the only things outcome-feedback demonstrably improves — while refusing the version that destroys the evidence stream. One more consequence the operator should weigh, because it bears on his actual goal: **if the aim is Phaethon eventually holding live capital, freezing is the *fast* path, not the slow one** — only a policy that holds still long enough to accumulate an attributable forward record can ever justify capital; a continuously-adapting policy resets its own evidence clock with every adjustment and therefore never arrives.

## 1. The steelman — what outcome-feedback would plausibly improve, with mechanism

Taking the operator's argument at full strength, three candidate improvements survive scrutiny as *mechanistically real*, not just "learning is good":

**1a. Conviction calibration — the strongest point.** If Phaethon's high-weight positions hit at the same rate as its low-weight ones, its confidence is uninformative and its sizing is destroying risk-adjusted return. The mechanism by which feedback fixes this is well-established and not speculative: calibration is the one cognitive quantity that reliably improves with outcome feedback — it is the documented core of forecaster training (Tetlock's superforecasting work), and it works precisely because the learner is shown the *gap between stated confidence and realized frequency*, a meta-level fact. An agent that never sees its resolution data cannot discover it is overconfident in a category. This is a genuine cost of total freezing.

**1b. Thesis-category pruning.** If "turnaround" theses resolve at 25% while "capacity-expansion" theses resolve at 60%, category-level feedback could retire a bad thesis class. Mechanism: base-rate updating over decision *types*, not picks — legitimate in principle, and exactly the kind of pattern a human reviewer might miss in free-text journals but a structured ledger surfaces. The binding constraint is sample size, quantified in §3.

**1c. Regime-drift detection — real concern, wrong channel.** A frozen prompt written in an AI-capex boom keeps its priors after the cycle turns. But note what this argument actually requires: awareness that *the world* changed — and Phaethon already legitimately receives market data, prices, fundamentals, and (post evidence-pack) verified point-in-time facts. Rates moving, breadth narrowing, a sector rolling over — all visible in permitted inputs without a single byte of own-P&L. The steelman conflates "responsive to the world" (allowed, already designed) with "responsive to my own outcomes" (the contested channel). Own-P&L adds, on top of world-data, mostly a sample-of-one noise term about *self* — the drift signal it uniquely carries is 1a/1b's calibration data, which routes through the boundary mechanism below. So 1c is answered by better inputs, not by outcome-learning; only 1a and 1b genuinely require the outcome channel.

## 2. The most conservative learning design that could work

Designed with the same rigor as any Olympus signal:

- **Unit of learning: meta-level only.** Categories (thesis types, override behavior, sizing tiers), never individual picks. "AVGO lost, avoid AVGO" is noise-memorization; "override-to-hold worsened outcomes in 8 of 9 cases" is a testable pattern.
- **Sample size, honestly computed.** Detecting a hit-rate difference between two thesis categories at 55% vs 45% needs ≈380 resolved trades *per category* for conventional power — unreachable, ever, at this book. Even a gross difference (65% vs 35%) needs ≈40 per category. Calibration-curve statements need ~30–50 *resolved* predictions to say anything with usable CI. Phaethon A holds ~18 sticky positions; a realistic closing rate is ~2–4/month → **~12–25 closed trades per 6 months, split across 4–6 thesis categories → 2–5 observations per category per half-year.** The learnable set at any given review is therefore: at most one or two *coarse* facts (aggregate calibration; one lopsided category at generous thresholds; a strongly one-sided behavioral pattern like override direction). The system's own constitution already encodes this principle: `MIN_OBSERVATIONS_FOR_CALIBRATION: 20`.
- **Statistical bar per lesson:** a lesson is adoptable only if it clears a pre-registered test at its n — e.g., binomial p < 0.05 against the null of 50% for directional patterns, or a calibration gap whose CI excludes zero. Lessons failing the bar are *recorded as candidates* and re-tested when n grows; they are not adopted early.
- **Validating the learning process itself (not the trades):** every adopted lesson must ship with a **falsifiable forward prediction** ("after removing turnaround theses, category-mix hit-rate rises; override rate falls below 20%"), logged in the ledger, resolved at the following review. The learning process is validated exactly as any signal: by whether its outputs' forward predictions resolve true above chance across review cycles. Note the honest implication: the validation unit is the *review cycle*, so speeding up reviews does not speed up validation of the learner — at any cadence, proving the learning process works takes years. There is no cadence that shortcuts this.

## 3. The reckoning — does the conservative version differ in kind from the boundary ceremony?

**No. Strip the framing and it collapses exactly.** "Learn only at long intervals, only from adequate samples, only meta-level patterns, with human review, versioned policy, and out-of-sample validation of the update" — every clause is the cohort-boundary ceremony, already designed, already using outcomes to inform revisions. The operator's real ask, once the "learning system" framing is removed, is **"do the boundaries faster and feed them better."** That is a legitimate ask, and half of it should be granted:

- **Feed them better: yes, fully.** The lessons ledger (below) turns the boundary review from impressionistic to statistical. This was underspecified in my prior design; the operator's pushback improves it.
- **Faster: only as fast as the trades permit, and here is the number.** Signal per review scales with closed trades since the last review. At ~2–4 closures/month: a **quarterly** review sees ~6–12 closures — below the constitution's own 20-observation calibration floor, so *any* revision at that cadence has a better-than-even chance of tuning to noise on the very categories it targets. A **~6-month-equivalent** review sees ~12–25 — marginal but workable for one or two coarse lessons at the §2 bar. Monthly or continuous sees per-category n of 0–2 — pure noise, guaranteed. Therefore the defensible rule is **event-triggered, not calendar-triggered: a boundary review becomes eligible when ≥25 trades have closed since the last review, with a 13-week calendar floor** (the floor prevents a burst of quick exits from gaming an early review; the n-trigger prevents a dead-calm period from forcing a content-free one). If Phaethon trades faster in future, reviews legitimately accelerate — cadence earned by information, which is the defensible core of the operator's instinct.

## 4. The pattern this operator's own history names

The sibling programme's record — **80+ strategy iterations, 1,020+ backtests, one marginal validated survivor** — is not a story about insufficient learning. It is the *canonical outcome of too-frequent outcome-based adjustment*: each failure informed the next redesign, each redesign was fit partly to the noise of the last sample, and the loop only produced a survivor once pre-registration forced it open and made iteration expensive. Continuous or frequent P&L-based adjustment of Phaethon is **the same pattern wearing an LLM costume, and the costume makes it worse, not better**, for three mechanical reasons: (i) the statistics are indifferent to what's doing the learning — per-category n of 2–5 is uninformative whether the learner is a human, a regression, or a transformer; (ii) an LLM is a *superb rationalizer* — it will produce a fluent, confident, plausible narrative for every noise-driven adjustment, removing the friction (fatigue, embarrassment, effort) that eventually stopped the human version of the loop at 80 iterations; a cron-driven LLM version has no such brake; (iii) LLM-specific failure content: fed its own recent losses, a language model reliably reproduces the human behavioral patterns saturating its training data — loss-aversion talk, recency-chasing, "cutting the losers" right before mean reversion — i.e., it doesn't just tune to noise, it tunes to noise *in the statistically worst documented directions*. And one system-level cost that outweighs everything: continuous adaptation destroys window interpretability, which destroys the forward record, which is **the only path Phaethon has to live capital**. The frozen policy is not the obstacle to Phaethon improving; it is the instrument that makes improvement provable.

## 5. The recommended mechanism, its guardrails, and its kill criterion

**What replaces the bright line: nothing — the bright line stays.** `NO_LIVE_PNL_LEARNING` continues to mean: no outcome data in any prompt/context, no mid-window policy change, no automated adjustment, ever. What is *added* is a defined, named, governed channel that was always implicit and is now explicit and improved:

**`OUTCOME_LEARNING_VIA_BOUNDARY_ONLY`** — the ceremony, upgraded:
1. **Trigger:** review eligible at ≥25 closed trades since last review AND ≥13 weeks elapsed. (Both; not either.)
2. **Input — the lessons ledger:** the consistency audit and journal analysis emit, per review, every candidate lesson *with its n, test statistic, and CI* — including the ones that fail the bar, marked "insufficient — carried forward." No lesson enters human review without its arithmetic attached.
3. **Adoption bar:** per-lesson pre-registered test passed (§2); human ratifies; **change budget: maximum 2 adopted lessons per revision** — wholesale rewrites are structurally impossible, which is what keeps v_{n} → v_{n+1} interpretable.
4. **Forward prediction:** every adopted lesson logs a falsifiable prediction resolved at the next review. The ledger is append-only and hash-chained like everything else.
5. **Firewall unchanged:** all of this is human-side machinery; nothing in it touches Phaethon's context. The model learns only by receiving a new frozen prompt at a boundary, exactly as before — just a boundary whose timing is earned and whose content is statistical.

**Evidence the guardrail is working:** adopted lessons' forward predictions resolve true at a rate credibly above 50% across cycles; the prompt-version series is monotone in content (lessons stick); adherence/calibration metrics in the consistency report improve across versions.

**Kill criterion for the learning mechanism itself** (pre-registered, same discipline as every other component): the boundary-learning channel is **suspended — reverting to annual calendar reviews with the ledger continuing observation-only** — if either occurs across any 4 consecutive review cycles: **(a)** ≥50% of adopted lessons' forward predictions resolve false, or **(b)** any lesson is adopted, reversed, and re-adopted (oscillation — the signature of tuning to noise). Suspension is logged, and reinstatement requires its own re-registration with a diagnosis of what failed.

**What research would settle the residual question, and what result decides it** (owed, since the verdict is a middle ground rather than a pole): the open empirical question is whether Phaethon's boundary-adopted lessons actually carry information — and the ledger *is* that experiment, pre-registered by construction. After 4 review cycles (~2 years at current trade frequency): forward-prediction resolution credibly above chance → the channel is validated and could cautiously widen (e.g., change budget to 3); resolution at or below chance, or oscillation → the kill criterion fires and the honest conclusion is that even slow outcome-learning adds nothing here beyond what world-data inputs provide. Either result is a real answer; the mechanism is built so the question cannot stay unfalsifiable.

## What changed my mind, and what didn't

**Changed:** boundary cadence should be information-triggered, not calendar-fixed — the operator is right that a learning system's review rhythm should follow its data, and my prior fixed-window framing conceded too little to that. Also changed: the review input needed to be a statistical artifact (the ledger), not a human impression over raw journals; "the ceremony, fed properly" is a materially better design than what I previously specified, and the pushback produced it. **Unchanged, with the mechanism now shown rather than asserted:** continuous or frequent outcome-adjustment fails on arithmetic (nothing to learn from at per-category n of 2–5), fails on this operator's own demonstrated pattern (the 80-iteration loop, now frictionless), and fails against the operator's own goal (an unfrozen policy can never accumulate the record that justifies live capital). The rule was never "no learning." It was always "learning at the speed the evidence actually arrives" — and the evidence, at this book's trade frequency, arrives about twice a year.
